# 0157. US-124 managers as built: a manager is a worker record that runs the business, a style that sets what a kind has, a half-made mis-set day, a share of the profit and a report

- Status: Accepted (the owner approved Preview 16 and gaps 5 to 8, 2026-11-21 and 2026-11-22; the story is for the owner's review)
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Deciders: Keyur Patel
- Related ADRs: 0027, 0069, 0081, 0082, 0153, 0155, 0156

## Context

US-124 (spec 028 §3.2): hand a business to a manager so your hours are free and learn what that costs. Four gaps were closed with the owner before code (ADR 0155, gaps 5 to 8). This ADR records what was built and the small rules the spec left open.

## Decisions (as built)

1. **A manager is a `Worker` with a `ManagerRecord`** (style, target, misses, days, report). He is hired, paid upfront, renewed, asked for a raise, poached and sick like any worker (spec 015), so none of that flow was rebuilt. `PeopleState.WorkerAt` is the worker who works the business (never the manager); `ManagerAt` is the manager. Saved from schema 23, hashed only once a manager exists (goldens and every older fixture unchanged).
2. **One post on the board for the town** (place −2; −1 is the masons' tab): you post it once (the usual fee), your townspeople apply at once, migrants come at the usual pace; you choose the business when you make the offer (`HireManager`). A manager's skill is the crafting skill (a townsperson's own). His asked wage follows his skill and the price index from a market wage of 4,000 (`managers.json` `wageBase`). *Why:* a manager is about running a business, not farming; one post is what Preview 16 showed.
3. **Gap 5, what a style sets:** every kind may have a manager. At 6:00 each morning, before the stalls fill and the makers start, he sets: a shop its price factor (1.00, 0.95 or 1.05 of the reference) and quality; a stall its day's stock (the plan's day times 1 or 2); the mill its fee (the reference fee times the same factor); a bakery or the sweet shop days of inputs bought ahead (1 or 2, bought at the import price only as far as the business's cash goes, never past the overdraft); a kiln nothing but the hire rule and mis-set days. What he sets has no button on the sheet: its row says "Set each morning by …", so a change of yours is never undone silently.
4. **Gap 6, taking over:** he takes your place at once (your hours go back to the farm); a worker already there stays. With nobody at work he posts the job on his first morning (no fee: it is his word) and each morning picks from the applicants by his style (the cheapest, or the most skilled) and offers what his style offers (10% less, the asked wage, 10% more); a worker who countered gets the asked wage next morning; he offers only what the business's cash can prepay. His low offers do not cost your employer reputation (the worker remembers it as a low hire; you did not make it). You cannot run a business yourself while it has a manager (`HasManager`); let him go first.
5. **Gap 7, the mis-set day:** one seeded draw a business a day (stream `Managers`) at his stars' chance (20%, 15.5%, 11%, 6.5%, 2%), moved by his mood (a glad manager mis-sets a little less, one sour after misses a little more, within 0 and 100%). On a mis-set day only half is made or sold (`missOutput` 0.5): a stall's hourly service, a maker's or the mill's grinding hours, a kiln's firing. One star loses about a tenth of the sales, five about a hundredth, as the spec says. A manager sick or on a course sets nothing that day.
6. **His pay:** the base wage upfront for the rest of the season from the business's cash (on its overdraft if need be, checked by the command), renewed at each close; at the close a tenth of the profit before his share when positive, taken before the tax (a manager never turns a profit into a loss; the tax is on what is left). Severance when let go is 10% of his wage after his first season, as a worker's. A manager of a closed business is let go with it. The Money page's next-close list includes his base wage.
7. **The target and the report (spec "a target you set, profit or growth"):** the goal is **last season's profit plus 5%** (profit target) or **last season's sales plus 10%** (growth target); for a new business, the plan's; in his first season a profit goal is last season's profit less his pay, so he is judged on what he adds; a goal is never below 0. At each close (after tax, before the books close) he reports met or missed against the profit the books show. Two misses in a row lower his mood by 0.1 for the season (spec 015 §3.4); a refused raise while underpaid and he leaves at the next close (ADR 0082). The report sheet shows the target against the result, what he changed and why, his mis-set days and pay, with a *Why?* on each line.
8. **Gap 8, managed against hired:** the spec's "managed profit at least the hired profit" is **measured and reported**, not enforced. The sheet shows his pay beside what the season made; the balance pass reports the honest gap and tunes the wage or the styles without re-approval (ADR 0027). A managed business counts as passive income (the Money page's passive line), as a hired one does.
9. **Screens:** the board's *Manager* tab (applicants with stars, style and a sentence), an offer that names the business first (a business with a manager is *Taken*), the business's *Manager* line on Today (with *Report* once one exists), his section in Settings (target, this season's goal and so far, pay, days mis-set, last close, *Let go* with the confirm), and the report sheet. Five *Why?* sources: his pay, his style, the mis-set days, the target, what a miss costs.

## Consequences

- Every number a manager touches has a *Why?* in words; the style and the mis-set chance are in `content/managers.json`.
- The mis-set effect is a halving of the day's work, not a loss of already-made sales: it works the same on every kind and needs one place in each hot loop (the mill and makers' capacity, the stalls' capacity, the kiln's rate).
- "Days of inputs ahead" for the bakery is a real, small mechanic (cash tied up, the then price); its worth is measured in the balance pass.
- Left for the sprint-end balance pass: managed against hired for each kind; whether 4,000 and a tenth is right for the Township's bakery and dairy; the exploit bot that hires and dismisses a manager every season to reset a first-season goal.
