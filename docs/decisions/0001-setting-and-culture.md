# 0001. Setting and culture: Indian-inspired fictional river valley

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Deciders: Keyur Patel
- GDD sections: Vision and pillars, Time and world simulation (Seasons), Economy, Art, audio and feel, Open decisions and risks

## Context

The GDD left the setting open, with two options: a fictional, culturally neutral river valley, or an Indian-inspired village and town. Art, names, the crop list, festivals and seasons all depend on this choice.

The GDD content already leans Indian. It includes a monsoon season, paneer, curd, ghee, jaggery, tea stalls, tempos, a banyan-tree square, Hindi as the first localisation after English, and an open question about rupee-scale prices.

## Options

1. **Fictional, culturally neutral valley.** This has the broadest appeal and needs no cultural-accuracy review. However, much existing content would need rewriting, and the result would look generic in a crowded genre (Township, SimCity BuildIt, Stardew Valley).
2. **Indian-inspired, fictional valley.** This keeps the existing content and gives the game a distinct identity, plus a natural home market and Hindi audience. It needs a cultural-accuracy review, and it risks stereotypes if the content is handled carelessly.

## Decision

Option 2. The setting is an **Indian-inspired but fictional** river valley. Place names are invented, and the game uses no real geography, real political parties, real religious figures or real brands. Places of worship and festivals are shown in a generic, inclusive way, with no single community favoured.

## Consequences

- The four seasons are named **Spring, Summer, Monsoon, Winter**, so the GDD's "Monsoon / Autumn" becomes "Monsoon".
- Crops, goods, festivals, names and art direction draw on Indian village and small-town life.
- A cultural-accuracy reviewer joins the pre-launch expert review, alongside the finance educator.
- The coin-scale decision is still open, but this setting makes rupee-like magnitudes a natural option.
- Localisation stays English first, then Hindi. Every string still goes in a string table.
