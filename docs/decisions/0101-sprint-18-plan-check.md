## Context

Sprint 18 builds spec 019 (F14.3 statements, F14.4 the hub and milestones) and holds the Phase 2 playtest. Before any code, each story, task and acceptance criterion was checked against spec 019, ADRs 0078 and 0083, the GDD and the code after Sprint 17 (PR #23). As it stands:

- Every closed season's report is kept for good (`FinanceState.Reports`), with its profit and loss and every part of net worth; the ledger keeps 4 seasons of entries.
- *Your season* (US-221) lists only loans, living and hands' renewals under *Next season, already due*, while the close also charges the warehouse's rent, water (power, the canal's fee, drip's upkeep), the school's upkeep and machines (lease, diesel, upkeep).
- There is no savings deposit, no account 1020 and no interest income account.
- Only five actions ask before they act (letting a hand or a worker go, selling an animal, clearing an orchard, changing an order). *Sell parcel* on an empty bought field sells the land at 80% of its price in one tap: the owner lost 4 acres that way in four weeks of play.
- Save schema is 15. Phase 2's gate reads "A week of play" in `epics.md`, and the playtest had no story.

Eleven gaps came out of it, and the owner added two requests to the first story.

## Decisions

1. **Every amount due (G1, a US-221 bug).** One sim function lists everything the next close takes from your cash, in the close's order: each loan's payment (interest and principal), the family's living, each hand's renewal, the warehouse's rent, water, the school's upkeep, each machine's lease, diesel and upkeep, each priced as the close prices it. *Next season, already due* and the emergency fund both read it. The short line names the first thing that would go unpaid ("the borewell's pump would stand idle"). Below your total, each business's own charges at the close (its worker's wage, its loan payment, its pitch's rent) are listed with "its cash covers it" or "short by X: put cash in", outside your total because the business pays them. `MoneyViewTests.NextSeasonDue` covers a town with every kind. Story **US-235**.
2. **The savings deposit only with your consent (G2).** Account **1020 Bank deposit**; interest 1.5% a season to **4400 Interest on savings** (*Other money*, not farm profit), added at each close before the statements, so it compounds.
   - **A day's warning.** At 00:00 of a season's last day, when the close would come up short, an alert (and, while away, a notification held to the end of quiet hours) says what's short and what would go unpaid, with its levers: **Pay from my savings**, **Sell from the store**, **People** (let a hand go), **Borrow**. Something done that day that makes it short again raises the alert again.
   - **Nothing leaves the deposit without consent.** A yes on the card covers that one close. The savings sheet has a standing choice, *If cash runs short at the close*: **Ask me** (the default) or **Always pay from my savings** (a bank's standing instruction, for a close you'll be away for). With consent, the shortfall comes from the deposit in the close's order, and the season report and the away card say so. Without it, the close runs as now: what can't be paid goes unpaid in its order, and the deposit is untouched.
   - *Next season, already due* counts cash only; when the deposit could cover the gap it says so, with **Pay from my savings**.
   - The deposit is never collateral (lenders' room ignores it) and never seized at a foreclosure, as cash in hand isn't.
   - Story **US-236** (moved out of US-135).
3. **The cash flow statement's groups (G3).** Each cash movement goes in the group of what the money went to or came from (the other side of its entry):
   - **Your work:** every income and expense account (gifts to the village, interest paid and earned included) and working capital (inputs into the store, wages and living paid ahead, the supermarket's payment, interest payable).
   - **What you bought, sold or saved** (renamed): land, buildings (the godown too), machines, trees and orchards, animals, cash put into or taken back from a business, and the savings deposit on its own row. A sale's whole price sits where the thing was bought; its gain or loss is in the profit and loss.
   - **Borrowing:** loans taken, principal repaid (interest stays under *Your work*), foreclosure payments.
   - The close's own payments belong to the new season, as on *Your season*; each statement's opening and closing cash are *Your season*'s.
4. **What's kept (G4).** No new lists: each season's report gains its cash flow rows by group, its *Your people* rows (wages against the market wage, who left and why, each worker's output, reputation then and its change) and the deposit as a line of net worth (save schema 16). Every report stays kept, as now (about 50 KB after a year of real play); the screens show the last 8. A schema-15 town's past seasons open at once with their profit and loss and balance sheet, and the trend draws from them; the cash flow rows and *Your people* start at the next close, a past season showing "Kept from your next season on" with its cash summary. **Nothing moves without the player seeing it (owner):** each row's *Why?* lists every movement behind it (day, what, from or to whom, amount); the ledger keeps 8 seasons of entries (`retainSeasons` 4 → 8; the save's size is measured and brought to the owner if large); there is never an *Other* row, and a test fails if any movement is unsorted or the rows don't add to the closing cash to the coin, every season of every bot run; every movement the close makes by itself is on that season's report and the away card.
5. **Where the statements are seen (G5).** One season page with three tabs as the mill's Books, **Profit and loss | Cash flow | Balance sheet**, each line beside the same season last year ("First Winter" until then) with a *Why?* naming the biggest change; the profit and loss keeps the advisor's line and ends with *Your people*. It opens at each close (as the season report now), from *Last season*'s **See the season report**, and from **Your books**, which lists the last 8 closed seasons (season, profit, cash at the end, net worth) and has no "this season so far": this season is *Your season*, now is *Your wealth*, and its header says so. *Your year* opens after the season page at each Winter close, before Dadi's letters and the Codex toast.
6. **The hub's numbers (G6).**
   1. The accountant's debt note fires at the risky band (60%), the same number as the hub's, read from `finance.json` ("over 60% of what you own").
   2. Passive income and the emergency fund divide by living at the plan you're on, never under the basic plan (18,000 a season × the index): a family eating from the store would otherwise have no living cost.
   3. Passive income is rent received (families' houses and your godown let), interest on savings, and the season's net profit of each business with a hired worker at the close, a loss counting against it; the ratio never shows under 0%.
   4. The emergency fund is (cash + deposit) ÷ decision 1's list, one decimal rounded down, amber under 1.
   5. Net worth's trend restates each past season in today's coins, its *Why?* giving both ("+8% in today's coins; +12% as counted then; prices rose 4%"). A past season's price index is recomputed from the town's seed (the inflation draw is seeded by season), so older towns' trends are exact with nothing stored. The statements stay as counted then: they are a record.
7. **Milestones (G7).** *Twice what you inherited* compares with the inheritance in today's coins; the inheritance is the net worth of a fresh game from the town's own seed and content, computed once and stored (schema 16). *First lakh saved* stays 1,00,000 as counted. At an older town's first close after the update, each milestone its kept reports show was reached is recorded with the season it really happened, in one toast ("4 milestones from your past seasons"); what the reports can't show waits for its moment. Several at once share one toast. *Debt-free* counts your own loans only and fires when the last closes by being repaid; a foreclosure's write-off doesn't count. Story US-136, 3 points.
8. **The playtest town and build (G8).** `VillageBot` (credit, homes first) plays a new game to the close after its third household (about day 14: Hamlet, 3 of 5 households with a home loan, a hand, the cattle, no business, no school), leaving each Village goal open and about 2–2.5 weeks to the Village with credit. The town is shown to the owner before it goes to the phone. The balancer writes the file when run on the Mac (`playtest-save`), from the build's commit, so importing it straight away loses minutes. The owner exports his own town first and imports it back after. A **Release build** (no developer panel) with Sprint 18's screens; the play log on from the import; a diary line a day. It runs until the Village or 3 weeks. Story **US-237**.
9. **The Phase 2 gate (G9)**, split as Phase 1's (ADR 0047):
   - **Part A**, at the playtest's end: A1 the bots' pace (`GateTests.Village`: homes first 28–40 days with credit, any order ≤ 50; saving 50–64, any order ≤ 80); A2 the owner reaches the Village from the day-14 town within 3 weeks, or the log and diary show why; A3 a session every day, the diary's reason not only "testing" on most days; A4 no blocker (ADR 0047's list plus a statement that doesn't add up, a cash movement not shown, savings taken without consent); A5 at three closes the owner writes, from the screens alone, why cash moved differently from profit, passing when the biggest reason matches the biggest *why your cash moved differently* row, and once what *Your year* told him; A6 the Release smoke list, both frame-time p95s, golden replays on the phone, CI green.
   - **Part B**, before Launch with Phase 1's: fresh players explain profit versus cash from the screens unprompted.
   - **A part A fail:** tuning in data with the bots re-run, or a rule change brought for approval; Phase 3's code waits, its spec writing may go on.
10. **Length and points (G10).** **Sprint 18: 2 weeks, 26 Apr – 9 May 2027, 36 points:** US-235 8 (every amount due 2, confirm cards 4, *Your land* 2), US-137 8 → 10, US-135 8 → 7, US-236 5, US-136 2 → 3, US-237 3. **Sprint 19: the playtest, up to 3 weeks, 10–30 May 2027, 6 points plus fixes:** US-238 the playtest (3), US-239 tune and the gate decision (3); Phase 3's spec writing (docs only, at most 2 specs to review) may run alongside; no Phase 3 code before the gate decision. The order of work and the cut order are in `sprints.md`; US-235, the deposit's consent and reconciling statements are never cut.
11. **Stale statements (G11)** fixed in this commit: `epics.md`'s Phase 2 gate; F14.3's schema 15 (16) and points; F14.4's stories and points; F10.2 for the playtest; spec 019's sections listed above and its note that *rent you pay* has no source (the warehouse's rent has, since Sprint 16); spec 008; the GDD; `sprints.md`'s rows; `STATUS.md`'s open questions (Phase 2 part B). ADR 0078's "schema 14" is kept as history (ADR 0083's convention). `retainSeasons` and the debt note's threshold change with their stories.

### The owner's requests in US-235 (2026-11-11)

12. **Confirm cards, as Hay Day.** Everyday play stays one tap: sowing, harvesting, *Collect all*, picking, selling to the trader, delivering an order, the mandi trip, buying inputs and medicine, hiring labourers for the day, spraying, the garden, settings and orders, moving cash between you, your savings and your businesses, a reference check.
   - **Card B, a normal tap** (spending on something that lasts): buying land; building a family house, a hut, the school, a shed; founding a business; buying, leasing or hiring a machine; the pump, a borewell, channels, drip; buying the godown or renting warehouse space; planting an orchard or a tree; buying an animal; taking or restructuring a loan; offering a season's contract; a course; an adult class.
   - **Card C, hold 1 second** (selling, closing, destroying, ending): selling land, a machine or an animal; giving a leased machine back; selling an orchard's fruit on the trees; closing a business; clearing a growing crop or an orchard; removing a tree; letting a hand or a worker go.
   - Every card shows its numbers: what it costs or fetches, a quick sale's discount, a loan repaid first, what goes with it, and what you'd pay or receive. *Keep* or *Not now* is the big safe button. "This can't be undone" on card C.
   - The rule is spec 008's (§3.15), so later actions follow it; `ConfirmTests.EveryCommand` lists every player command and fails if one isn't sorted as everyday, tap or hold.
13. **Your land.** Menu → *Buy land* becomes **Your land**, with two tabs. **Yours:** acres owned, their value at market and loans on land (each with *Why?*); inherited land first, "can never be sold or seized"; each bought parcel with its soil, acres, when bought, what's on it, any loan on it and its market value, **Show** and **Sell** (card C; greyed with the reason when something on it or a loan stops the sale). **For sale:** today's list, its *Buy* on card B. The field's own sheet loses *Sell parcel*; *Clear* stays there, on card C. The cards and *Your land* are previewed in the game's style before code.
