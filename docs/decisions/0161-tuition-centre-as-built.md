# 0161. US-244 the tuition centre as built: pupils from both your households and the old village, fees a day, stars that set take-up, skill for your children

- Status: Accepted (the owner closed gaps 14 to 17 before code, 2026-11-22; the story is for the owner's review)
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Deciders: Keyur Patel
- Related ADRs: 0069, 0074, 0116, 0153, 0155, 0157, 0159, 0160

## Context

US-244 (spec 028 §3.5, Preview 18): a tuition centre from the Township where richer households spend their schooling budget on evening classes and their children's skills grow. The spec set the capacity (20 a batch, two batches), the fee (800 a pupil a season, four presets) and the gain (+1 farming and crafting a season, capped at +10), but not who the pupils are, how the cap meets the adult course, when a fee is paid, or what the centre's stars do. Four gaps were closed with the owner before code.

## Decisions (as built)

1. **A new kind of business, not a shop.** `content/businesses.json` gets `tuition` (era Township, building 40,000, equipment 15,000, a tutor at 20,000 a season on the crafting skill, 15:00 to 19:00) with its own block (`TuitionDef`): two batches of 20, ages 7 to 17, fee 800, presets 1.0, 0.9, 1.1 and 1.2, start stars 2, take-up 40% to 100%, books and chalk 50 a pupil a season, +1 a season up to 10. It works like the kiln in the code (`IsTuition`, `TuitionRules`), reads no goods and has no shop sheet. The hiring board gets a *Tutor* tab by itself.
2. **Gap 14, pupils from both, skill for yours only.** Your households' children aged 7 to 17 come first, in household order; a household's schooling budget (5% of its income, already set at each close) pays for as many as it covers at the fee and each fee a household commits (here or at another centre) comes off it. Of the children a household can pay for a share are sent by one seeded draw (stars, below). The old village, a head-count with no citizens, fills the rest: its people × the share who are children × the centre's ages out of a child's years (a fifth of them 7 to 17 in the content: 27 children at 30 households), each household's budget over the fee, never more than its children, × the same take-up. A child is in one centre only; two centres share the children in id order. *Why:* an early Township has few children of its own, and a centre must be able to fill; the skill reward stays with the named children.
3. **Gap 15, separate caps, they stack.** A child's tuition is kept as `Citizen.TuitionSeasons` (schema 23, hashed only when used) and stops adding at 10 seasons; a child at the cap is not enrolled again. The adult course (+10 for 6,000) is its own, so a tutored child who becomes a hand can still be sent on a course.
4. **Gap 16, enrol at the close and on opening, pay by the day.** Pupils are enrolled when the centre opens (not a season later) and at each season's close, after last season's gain. A pupil keeps the place all season. The fee is **a day's share of the season's fee** (the season is 4 real days, so a quarter each evening) taken at the last hour of the pupil's batch (16:00 and 18:00), with the books and chalk; an evening with no tutor, a festival, a fire or a strike earns nothing and needs no refund. **The fee is fixed at enrolment** (`Business.Fee` holds the factor then), so a price raised after the pupils come in cannot be lowered again before the next enrolment to cheat; `SetTuitionFee` changes the next enrolment only.
5. **Gap 17, stars set take-up.** Stars start at 2 and move a tenth of the way a day (as the shops) to a target of `5 × (full × 3/5 + stayed × 2/5)`: *full* is pupils over places, *stayed* the share of your pupils who have sat a season already. Take-up is 40% of the children a family can pay for at no stars, rising evenly to all of them at five (64% at the start). The *Why?* says so in percentages.
6. **The skill gain.** At each close every pupil of yours who sat the season (the centre ran: it took fees) gains the content's +1 to farming and to crafting (never past 100) and a season of tuition. At 18 they come to the hiring board a better hand; the citizen card says "n seasons at a tuition centre" with a *Why?*. The old village's children gain nothing named.
7. **Built simplifications (to confirm).** (a) **An acre only, no pitch in the square.** The spec said "a pitch or an acre"; the square's pitches are the shops' (sprites, rent, customers by distance) and a classroom does not need them. (b) The manager's style sets the preset nearest to its price factor. (c) The *Orders* tab is hidden for the centre (it sells to families). (d) The centre's hours are fixed (the settings have the fee, not the hours): the batches are its hours.
8. **The plan.** Pupils the town would send now at the starting stars × the fee, less books, repairs, wear and the tutor (none when you run it). At today's town a hired centre plans a small loss and one you run a profit; a centre fills as its stars rise, which the plan's *Why?* says. Spec §3.5's targets (hired 3,000 to 8,000 a season, run by you 18,000 to 25,000) are for a full centre and are measured at the sprint-end balance pass.
9. **Screens** (Preview 18): the research, the plan, the placement ghost and the card are the shared ones; *Today* has pupils (yours and the old village's), a line a batch, the fees this season, stars with their take-up and the seasons the pupils have sat; *Settings* has the fee preset with when it takes effect; every number has a *Why?*. The building is a lime-washed classroom with a tiled roof, a verandah, a blackboard and benches (`tuition_s/e/n/w` and the building stage), rendered in Blender.
10. **Save schema 23** (hashed only when used): a centre's pupils (citizen ids), the old village's count and the season's fees; a child's seasons. The growth fixture holds a centre with enrolled pupils and a child with seasons.

## Consequences

- The Treasury test that removes the Township era must also move the tuition centre's era; the content check rejects an unknown era.
- Balance (sprint end): the centre's targets at a full house; whether 27 old-village children and your own households fill it; the 20% rule against the other businesses.
