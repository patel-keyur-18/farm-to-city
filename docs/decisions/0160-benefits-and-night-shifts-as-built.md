# 0160. US-258 benefits and night shifts as built: benefits paid upfront with the wage, a night shift as a second worker, "behind on the plan" days

- Status: Accepted (the owner closed gaps 12 and 13 before code, 2026-11-22; the story is for the owner's review)
- Date: 2026-11-22
- Deciders: Keyur Patel
- Related ADRs: 0069, 0082, 0125, 0153, 0155, 0157, 0159

## Context

US-258 (spec 028 §3.8): benefits a worker values at a cost, raising motivation and loyalty; a night shift for the businesses that make things, on the grid at a premium wage. Two gaps were closed with the owner before code: **gap 12** (how and when a benefit is paid, and what switching it mid-season does) and **gap 13** (the night shift needs a second worker, and nothing recorded "sold out before the day shift ended").

## Decisions (as built)

1. **Per employer.** A business gives its benefits to everyone it pays (day worker, night worker, manager); your farm gives its hands'. Four switches (`SetBenefit`): a meal (1,800), health cover (600, needs the clinic with its doctor in post), the festival bonus (1,000) and paid leave (900). Numbers are in `content/benefits.json`; costs are a worker a season at year-0 prices.
2. **Gap 12, paid upfront with the wage, off stops at once.** At each renewal the season's cost for every worker is paid from the employer's cash (a business from its own cash and overdraft; the farm from yours) with the wage, booked as `ledger.benefits` in the wage line. Switching on part-way charges the rest of the season at once; a worker hired part-way is charged the rest of it. Switching off stops the effect at once and refunds nothing. An employer that cannot pay at a renewal loses the benefits (the festival bonus, paid only on the day, stays on). The festival bonus is paid on the festival's day (`FestivalHeld`), to each worker, and raises your name by 1.
3. **Effects.** Motivation is added through the existing mood (meal +0.03, health +0.02, leave +0.03, the festival bonus +0.04 only in the season it was paid), still capped at 1.2 by the work rules. Health cover halves the chance of a sick day and pays the sick days itself, so no card. Paid leave takes 4 of a season's 90 days' work (output × 86 ÷ 90) and 0.10 of tiredness off at each renewal. Each benefit multiplies the poaching chance, and the chance a worker who would leave unhappy actually does (one seeded draw a season), by 0.85. Two or more kept through a close raise your name as an employer by 1 (once, however many employers). *Why:* spec 015's reputation and loyalty already exist; benefits only feed them.
4. **A Why? with "Does it pay?" in your numbers.** Each benefit's *Why?* shows its cost, the output it adds (for paid leave the output it loses: +3% motivation less the 4.4% of days not worked is a net **loss** of output), what it does to poaching with your wage and reputation. Spec §3.8: benefits do not pay in output alone.
5. **Gap 13, the night shift is a second worker.** The mill and the businesses that make by recipe (bakery, dairy, sweet shop) may start one (`SetNightShift`; a tap with the wage and what the business pays now). It needs the day worker, the **grid** (`UtilitiesBuilt` grid bit, a generator does not count) and the offer. The night worker is a `Worker` flagged `Night` (schema 23), a commuter at skill 50, at the role's wage × 1.25 (never under the minimum wage), the rest of the season paid now from the business's cash and each renewal upfront; the business's `WorkerAt` is still the day worker. **Built simplification (to confirm):** the worker is taken on at once from the commuters, not chosen from the hiring board; Preview 18 said "hired from the board".
6. **Hours.** 20:00 to 04:00, no overtime; the business is open for the night worker's capacity in those hours (production and the mill's own grinding; the mill's area grain still comes only in the village's hours). Output is capped by the day's plan (a recipe's mix, the mill's wheat a day), so the night adds hours, not customers. The night worker never rests by day: tiredness +0.07 a day to the usual maximum.
7. **Offered when the day shift is behind.** Nothing recorded "sold out", so each business counts, at the end of the day shift (the hour after its close), whether the plan went unmet (a recipe's product made below its mix; the mill's own wheat not all ground while wheat is in the market). The last 8 days are kept (hashed only when used). The night shift is offered when 3 of the last 8 were behind. *Why:* "sold out" needs a demand model the businesses do not have; "could not make what its own plan asked" is what extra hours can fix. The sheet says "Day shift behind on 3 of the last 8 days".
8. **Ending.** It stops when you stop it (severance as for workers), when the day worker goes, when the business is sold or closed (a sale pays it off with the day worker), or when the business cannot pay its next season; without the grid it does not work (the row says so). Accidents ×2 on a night shift are spec 029's (US-259).
9. **Screens** (Preview 18, screen 7): a *Benefits* section on a business's Settings tab (below its own settings) and on People for your hands, each row a switch with its cost and effect and a *Why?*, the *Kept: n of 4* line with poaching now, the *Night shift* row (offered, on, or why not). The Treasury's utility lines (tower, grid, generator and others) had no names in the strings: they crashed the department's *Why?* once built; added.
10. **Save schema 23** (hashed only when used): a business's benefit flags, last festival season and the behind-days record; your farm's benefit flags and festival season (in the Township's exit record); the Worker's `Night` flag. The growth fixture holds a bakery with a meal and paid leave and a night worker, and a farm meal.

## Consequences

- `GateTests.StackBenefits` and `GateTests.NightNoDemand` run at the sprint-end balance pass: all four benefits stacked must cost more than the output they add (paid leave costs output), and a night shift on a business the day shift already satisfies must lose money.
- Business loans and cash: benefits come out of the business's cash first, so a thin business that gives all four may lose them at a renewal; that is by design.
