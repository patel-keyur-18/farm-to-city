## Context

The Sprint 5 planning review (2026-10-19) checked the people stories (US-066 to US-072) against spec 006, specs 002–005 and 008, the map content and the code before any code was written. The owner also asked whether the Blender sprite pipeline (US-171) should start this sprint, since Blender is installed and the art is still placeholder. Ten gaps were found and decided one by one.

## Decisions

1. **Blender pipeline: a slice now, the rest in Sprint 6.** A new story US-183 (5 points, Sprint 5) builds the headless rig (`blender -b -P`, models built in Python, one fixed isometric camera and light rig, toon shading with a thick outline, PNGs imported with fixed settings) and renders the sprites Sprint 5 needs: the hand's hut (building and done), the hut and the well re-rendered, and three people (founder, hand, day labourer) in two facings (mirrored to four), with code-driven motion (bob, squash, pop) and the people on the map (decision 8). A **style checkpoint** comes first: three pilot renders shown to the owner as Editor screenshots before the rest. US-171 (Sprint 6) shrinks to the remaining re-render (crops, trees, garden, props, ground, seasonal variants, 3 points) plus the sheet and HUD icons rendered from the same models (1 point). **By the end of Sprint 6 every sprite on the map and every icon is a Blender render,** provided the owner approves the look at the Sprint 5 checkpoint. Frame animation (walk and hoe cycles) is a later story. Full US-171 now (29 points) was rejected: it would push the standing orders to Sprint 6 and risk re-rendering about 55 sprites if the style needs changes.
2. **Hands' huts stand on the edge of an inherited farm acre, not in the yard.** The one yard tile already holds the hut, the well, two garden beds and four tree places. Each inherited farm acre has one hut place on its edge (`LandDef.hutPlacesPerAcre` 1), away from its tree places; the yard holds no hands' huts and `yardBuildings` is dropped. At most `HandDef.maxHandHuts` (2) in Phase 1. Inherited acres can be neither sold nor foreclosed (ADR 0009), so a hut is never lost with its land; bought land may host huts from Phase 2. A hut doesn't change the acre's yield (about 200 sq ft of 43,560). `BuildHandHut(tileId)` is rejected with `NotInherited` (any other tile), `NoRoom` (the acre has a hut) or `TooManyHuts`; `NotYard` is gone. The placement ghost drags over the acres, green on a free place and red with the reason; a Confirm card shows the cost.
3. **Regulars are counted by time actually hired.** "A part day counts as one" let a player hire at 23:55 six times and get a regular with known skill for almost nothing. Each roster member now adds the seconds they were hired; a regular needs `regularThresholdDays` (6) full day-equivalents (6 × 86,400 s). Pay stays pro rata, which is realistic on the compressed clock (one game hour is about one real working day).
4. **Renewing a lapsed contract pays the whole season (back pay).** Draining cash before a close to force a lapse, then renewing two days later pro rata, saved half a season's wage. `RenewContract` within the grace now pays the full season's wage, the waiting days included (the hand stayed and is owed them); the days already passed are expensed at once. A contract still lapsed at the next season close ends and the hand leaves. A lapse saves nothing and loses the idle days' work.
5. **A day labourer's assigned task is "tend the fields".** In Phase 1, Care is the only work that uses hours, so the labourers' hours join the shared Care pool (spec 003 §3.6) and ADR 0022's "task assigned for the day" means exactly that. The hire row previews the day's Care in words (for example "Fair → Good") through `Simulation.Preview`. Tasks per field may come with Phase 2's jobs.
6. **Orders follow the player, and take time.**
   - The player always wins: an order acts only on a field that is ready or empty and never undoes the player's crop. When the player sows a different crop on an ordered field, the UI asks "Change Ramesh's order to potato?": Yes sends `SetStandingOrder` then `Sow`; No sows once and the order resumes after that crop. Harvesting an ordered field by hand is fine; the order re-sows at the next hour.
   - Each hand does at most `HandDef.orderActionsPerHour` (1) action (harvest, sow or pick) per hourly pass, in target order. On the map the hand walks to the field the order acts on. Orders don't take hours from the Care pool, as the founder's taps don't.
7. **Words and stars, not raw numbers.** Skill shows as stars (one per 20 points) and a band word from `ui.json` (Beginner < 35, Capable 35–54, Skilled 55–74, Expert ≥ 75). An applicant's range fills stars solid to its low end and faded to its high end ("Capable to Skilled"). Hours show as "tends about N acres" (the hand's hours ÷ a reference acre of wheat, 3 h a day, rounded to a half). Traits are a word with a short line. Coin amounts stay numbers. The exact figures stay in *Why?* (the sheet arrives in Sprint 7; the rows carry their `WhyKey`s now).
8. **Where hiring lives, and people on the map.**
   - A round **People** button bottom-right on the HUD, with a badge counting waiting applicants; in Sprint 7 it becomes a Menu entry and stays as a shortcut. Tapping a hand's hut (or a hand) opens the hand sheet; a hut being built shows its progress.
   - The People sheet: day labourers (one row per count with its cost and Care preview), hands, applicants (stars, trait, wage, Interview, Offer), Build a hand's hut, Post a job (market, above market). The hand sheet: contract and paid-until date, orders (Change, Remove), Renew or Let go (with the refund).
   - Orders are given from the map (a field or tree sheet row "Ramesh looks after this field", then the crop picker and the sell rule) and listed on the hand sheet. Sell rules in words: "Keep in store", "Sell at the market, not below 26 (today 28)" with − and + steps, "Sell to the trader at once".
   - The founder and the hands stand on fields being tended; a hand walks to the field an order acts on; day labourers walk in from the map edge when hired and leave at the daily pass. Presentation only, not sim state.
9. **A people-only *While you were away* card now.** On the first live `Sync` after an away advance, a card lists the people rows of spec 008 §3.9: what each hand did (harvests, sales, sowings, waiting reasons), day labour paid, contracts renewed or lapsed. It is built on the `Sim.View` away view model that US-083 (Sprint 7) extends with the rest. Each standing order also keeps its last action (action, time, quantity, amount) in state, so the hand sheet shows "Last done" at any time.
10. **Balance checks for people.** The competent `FarmerBot` hires like a good player (a hut at the end of the first Spring, with a small moneylender loan if needed; a market-wage job post; interview and offer to the best applicant; renewals; orders on its acres; all 6 acres from Summer) and checks spec 006 §9's hooks. Four stress bots join the gate checks, each at most 1.20 × the competent bot's median net worth over 2 years: a day-labour spammer, a hand churner (lets go in probation, posts above market for skill), a lapse gamer (proves decision 4) and an absentee (orders plus 72 h away advances; a floor check, at least 0.80). A bot outside its bound comes back to the owner as a new gap.

11. **The hand's wage is 30,000 a season (was 36,000); the day wage 9,375 (was 11,250).** Found by decision 10's gate checks after the review (owner, 2026-10-19): at 36,000 a founder who never hires ends about 8% richer after 11 years, because the extra acres sell into the same small valley market and the founder loses their spare-hour day labour. At 30,000 (about ₹10,000 a month with free housing; about ₹420 a real day for day labour) hiring pays modestly: +1% after 2 years, +7% after 11. 15,000–20,000 was considered and rejected: below rural minimum wages, and hiring would beat farming alone by 34–47%, so the decision (and the spec 006 lesson) would vanish; it doesn't speed up the Hamlet either (day 5.4 at every wage). **Hiring should feel more rewarding through realistic levers, never by underpaying:** more land to put hands on, Phase 2's processing and markets, and skill growth. ADR 0030's pacing line ("hire a hand (36,000)") reads 30,000 from now on.
